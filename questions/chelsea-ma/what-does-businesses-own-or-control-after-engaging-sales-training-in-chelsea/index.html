What Businesses Own After Training

Upon completing a sales training program, businesses should retain ownership of several key assets:

  • Customized Training Materials: These include guides, handbooks, and other instructional materials tailored to the business's unique sales environment.
  • Sales Methodologies: The specific processes and strategies developed during training become the intellectual property of the business, enabling them to train new hires in a consistent manner.
  • CRM Setup and Integration: Control over customer relationship management systems that have been tailored during training ensures ongoing utilization of best practices in sales.
  • Performance Metrics: The frameworks for measuring sales performance and accountability structures are established and owned by the business, allowing for continuous improvement.

What Remains with the Provider

While businesses gain substantial ownership of the assets mentioned, there may be elements that remain with the training provider. This could include:

  • Proprietary Training Techniques: Certain methodologies or techniques that the provider uses may not be fully transferable.
  • Ongoing Support and Consultation: The provider may retain the rights to offer ongoing coaching or consultative support, which can be invaluable for sustained success.

Negotiating Ownership Language

It's crucial for businesses in Chelsea to negotiate the terms of ownership upfront. Here are some tips for ensuring clarity in ownership:

  • Define Deliverables Clearly: Specify what will be delivered at the end of the training process. This includes written materials, recorded sessions, and any tools developed.
  • Ownership Clause: Include a clause that explicitly states what intellectual property the business retains after the engagement.
  • Clarify Support Terms: Understand the nature of ongoing support and what aspects of the training remain proprietary to the provider.
